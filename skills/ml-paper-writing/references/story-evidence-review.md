# Story, reverse outline and evidence review

Use for a substantial draft or review; keep narrow paragraph requests narrow. Read the relevant manuscript and evidence before judging its story.

1. Summarize the scientific question, supported contributions, main results and limits in a short story brief. Tie each result to its figure, table, equation or run record. Give each section a one-line purpose.
2. Reverse-outline the section: list each paragraph's actual message, its relation to the section claim, and the evidence or explanation that supports it. A paragraph can introduce motivation or an assumption without claiming an experimental result. Repair missing transitions, redundant messages and unsupported leaps; do not force every paragraph into a rigid template.
3. Check Abstract/Introduction/Conclusion claims against the evidence table. Ask whether the method answers the stated gap, baselines are comparable and sufficiently strong, controls isolate the claimed cause, and uncertainty and failure cases constrain the conclusion. State-of-the-art or generality claims need evidence under comparable conditions; error-bar separation alone is not a valid significance rule.
4. Separate citation identity from support for the nearby claim. Anchor findings to manuscript locations and source locations; label unresolved verification instead of asserting an error. Use official current venue rules only for a requested venue pass, recording required versus measured values and unverifiable items.
5. Rank findings by scientific consequence, then clarity and presentation. For each, provide the exact location, supporting evidence and smallest useful fix. Carry authorized revisions through build/render checks, preserving numerical values, quantifiers and scientific meaning. Leave unresolved author judgments explicit; do not invent results or require story approval before doing an already-authorized review.

Useful review questions cover contribution, clarity, experimental strength, evaluation coverage and method soundness. Keep the review report outside the manuscript unless the requested deliverable includes it.
